# Steven Dux's Proven Small-Cap Shorting Strategies: Gap-Up Short, Bounce Short, and First Red Day

### Overview

This episode features Steven Dux, a highly successful trader who transformed an initial $27,000 into over $50 million through precise shorting strategies. He shares three highly detailed and statistically-based shorting models tailored for small-cap stocks under $100-$200 million market cap with float sizes ranging mainly from 1 million to 50 million shares. The discussion blends trading statistics, psychology, and risk management principles, illustrating how strict criteria improve winning percentages and reduce emotional trading mistakes such as FOMO.

### 1. Gap-Up Short Strategy [00:03:19 – 00:19:34]

**Key Criteria:**

| Criterion           | Requirement                                      | Notes                                   |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Market Cap          | $1 million to $100 million                       | Over $200M is not tradable for this     |
| Float                | 1M to 50M                                       | Subsections: 1–2M (very low), 2–5M (mid), 5–10M (large) |
| Price                | Above $3                                        | Under $3 distorts statistics             |
| Gap Size             | At least +100%                                  | Often gaps even 1,000%                   |
| Volume (Pre-market) | Filter out if >50M pre-market                    | Volume 1:5 to 1:10 pre-market to daily volume ratio |
| Sector Exclusion     | Avoid Biotech, Energy, and Chinese Stocks       | These lower win rates and increase risk |

**Strategy Logic & Execution:**

- Stocks gap heavily in the morning, showing strong initial demand.
- Wait for consolidation post-gap, typically from 9:30 to 11:30 when volume concentrates.
- Enter short on the **first breakdown after consolidation**, with stop loss set above the consolidation high.
- Typical risk around 7%; average intraday fade is ~26%, generating a favorable risk/reward ratio of approximately 1:3.5.
- Winning percentage: **~75%+ annually**, with about 50-70 setups per year after filtering.
- Volume analysis is essential: estimate entire daily volume to anticipate crowded trades, avoid heavily crowded stocks with excessive volume, as this reduces success chances.

**Psychology & Risk Note:**

- Shorting crowded, high-volume stocks leads to frequent false breakouts.
- Precise sizing and criteria reduce the chance of being shaken out early.

### 2. Bounce Short Strategy [00:22:37 – 00:34:38]

**Key Criteria & Setup:**

| Criterion              | Requirement                                       | Notes                                       |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Market Cap            | Ideally under $200 million                        | Slightly more flexible than gap-up short    |
| Float                  | Under 50 million                                 |                                         |
| Price                  | Above $3                                         | Same as gap-up short                         |
| Significant Volume Day | One candle with ~30M+ volume, price spikes up   | Creates resistance level                      |
| Volume Post-Spike      | Lower volume compared to spike day               | Sell pressure builds at resistance          |

**Strategy Logic & Execution:**

- Identify a prior large volume spike candle (e.g., $5 price, 30M shares) followed by a flat consolidation for months.
- When price gaps up and approaches this resistance level, early sellers who are stuck from previous highs sell to break even.
- This emotional response leads to immediate selling pressure, creating an ideal short entry at or near the resistance/consolidation level.
- Ideal dollar volume block to short into: **$150 million+**, calculating as float shares × price at resistance.
- Volume ratios of current day vs. resistance volume matter greatly — larger ratio (e.g., 2:1 or more) increases conviction and win percentage.
- Winning percentage: **80-85%**, about 30 setups per year.
- Size limitations: max 10% of the float and 1% of daily volume to avoid breaking supply/demand dynamics.

**Variations:**

- Type 1: Gap up close to resistance with volume buildup; fade from spike is about 75%.
- Type 2: Volume builds gradually and bounces but fades back—lower reward of about 50%.

**Risk Management:**

- Large position sizes can distort the market, so scaling out along the fade is recommended rather than holding full size throughout.

### 3. First Red Day Strategy [00:34:38 – 01:00:25]

**Key Criteria:**

| Criterion                            | Requirement                                            | Notes                                    |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Initial Market Cap                 | Up to $200 million                                    | Mostly small caps, but flexible          |
| Price                             | Above $3                                             | Maintains consistency                     |
| Parabolic Action                  | Price and volume increase over 3+ consecutive days    | Volume must rise each day without red days in between |
| Range                            | At least 300% increase over initial breakout in 3+ days or 1000% in 2 days | Significant price surge is necessary    |
| Volume & Candle Consistency       | Consecutive green days with increasing volume and no resets | A red or flat day resets count           |

**Strategy Logic & Execution:**

- Identify a rapid run-up (parabolic move) with multiple consecutive green days, demonstrating emotional buying pressure.
- On the **first red day following this run-up**, look for a red close with some intraday green movement (so sellers start taking control).
- Short entry is not rushed immediately on the red day; position starts small (~25%) due to high volatility and risk.
- The larger position is built the **following day** once volume and momentum confirm selling dominance and a breakdown begins.
- Use stop loss above consolidation, not necessarily highest spike.
- Winning percentage: **up to 90%**, although setups are less frequent (~5-10 per year).
- Position sizing must be very precise since the larger initial size increases risk dramatically.

**Psychological Insights:**

- Hedge funds and large players typically avoid pushing over 30% of market cap/float to maintain retail participation; once this limit is hit, the stock usually crashes dramatically.
- Understanding dollar volume thresholds by market cap is key to predicting when the top is reached.
- Avoid emotional mistakes and premature entries that cause losses during the run-up.

### Practical Trade Examples and Execution Notes [01:00:25 – 01:07:43]

- **Gap-Up Short Example (BIRD)**: Stocks trading over 50 million shares pre-market often become crowded and less reliable to short due to sustained volume and strength; better to short on post-crowded-day fades or the following day when volume dries up.
- **Low Float Variation**: For stocks with float under 2M and volume rotating >15x float, wait for a 50% retracement bounce signal before shorting to avoid dangerous volatility.
- **Bounce Short Example**: A stock rising from $2 to $6 with a huge volume spike (~100M) forms a clear resistance; subsequent consolidation and lower volume days offer an entry point as it fails to break resistance.
- **First Red Day (BYND and Silver examples)**: Shows the real-world necessity of strict criteria to avoid premature or false entries; volume, candle structure, and price range must confirm the pattern.
